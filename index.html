A Cost–Quality Trade-off Study of Lightweight Generative Adversarial Networks for 64 × 64 Hematological Synthesis

Rodrigo F. A. P. Oliveira · Carmelo J. A. Bastos Filho

Escola Politécnica de Pernambuco, Universidade de Pernambuco, Recife, Brazil

Most comparisons of lightweight medical GANs pit one custom system against StyleGAN2-Ada, two at a time. That design can rank the two, but it cannot test whether the heavy baseline belongs on the cost–quality curve at all. We add a third lightweight system and find that it does not.

What the study found

The result

8 of 8 classesFastGAN-64 attains the lowest FID in every class, faster and on less inference VRAM than StyleGAN2-Ada. Parameter count is the one axis it concedes.

The method

≈ 62 GPU-hoursA two-system comparison could not have shown this. With the released patch, adding a third system costs about a weekend of compute.

The setting

8 GB consumer GPUEvery number was produced on a single NVIDIA RTX 3050, at roughly a millisecond per generated image.

Scope of the claim

The result holds at the operating point measured: BloodMNIST 64 × 64, eight classes, 88 training runs, one consumer GPU, with each system under its own published reference recipe rather than a matched compute budget. It is not a claim about the lightweight regime in general, and nothing here contradicts the documented advantages of StyleGAN2-Ada at 256 × 256 and above. FID and KID are computed on Inception-V3 features trained on natural images, so no metric reported here establishes clinical realism, and downstream classifier utility was not measured.

Reproducing the numbers

The repository contains the FastGAN-64 patch (the reference implementation hardcodes im_size >= 256), the matched FID 200v200 protocol, the robustness analyses added for the camera-ready version, and the per-class CSV results behind every table in the paper. See the README.
